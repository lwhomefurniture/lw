Bathroom Vanity Size Guide: 15 to 84 Inches

Vanity width changes more than appearance. It affects sink configuration, storage, circulation and how balanced the room feels. This guide compares the sizes currently represented in the LW Home Furniture vanity collection so homeowners, designers and trade buyers can narrow the right format before choosing a style.

How to choose the right width

15–24 inches: maximize a small footprint

These compact widths are useful where floor area is limited but you still want dedicated storage and a finished furniture look. A narrow vanity can work particularly well in a powder room, guest bath or a tight wall niche. See the 15-inch Solstice Teak Vanity and the 24-inch Ellington Natural Oak Vanity.

30–36 inches: versatile single-sink sizing

Thirty- and thirty-six-inch vanities offer a useful balance between compact dimensions and everyday storage. They are available in both freestanding and floating formats, so the visual weight can be adjusted to suit the room. Compare the 30-inch Sculpt Walnut Floating Vanity with the 36-inch Aurelia Ash Vanity.

48 inches: more counter space without going oversized

A 48-inch vanity gives a single user more drawer and countertop space while remaining suitable for many medium-size bathrooms. Floating models can make this width feel lighter by keeping the floor visible. Explore the 48-inch Linea Oak Floating Vanity.

60 inches: a flexible size for primary bathrooms

At 60 inches, both generous single-sink and double-sink configurations become practical. This size is common where two users need shared storage but the room does not call for a 72- or 84-inch cabinet. See the 60-inch Montara Ash Double Vanity and the 60-inch Riva Natural Oak Floating Double Vanity.

72–84 inches: double-sink storage for larger rooms

These wider vanities create a strong furniture presence and allow more separation between basins, drawers and cabinet zones. They suit larger primary bathrooms and project specifications that prioritize symmetrical storage. Compare the 72-inch Reed Ash Double Vanity with the 84-inch Aurelia Oak Double Vanity.

Floating or freestanding?

A floating vanity exposes more floor and creates a lighter architectural look. It can work especially well in contemporary spaces and makes floor cleaning easier. A freestanding vanity has a more furniture-like presence and can suit traditional, transitional or modern rooms depending on the door, drawer and leg design.

What trade buyers should confirm before ordering

For retail, builder and project orders, confirm finished width, cabinet depth, countertop thickness, basin type, plumbing clearance, finish, hardware, packaging and required quantities before production.
